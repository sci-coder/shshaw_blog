In a world full of distractions, mind and attention are split between so many ideas that we rarely are able to garner a force of thought to pierce a specific concept to depth, to reveal its nitty-gritty details. With the advent of social media, one-click away and cheap entertainment we cannot stop ourselves from getting hooked to our electronic screens. It doesn’t mean that these things are inherently bad. They have also brought a tremendous positive value to our lives. But have we ever taken a hiatus and reflected whether spending hours on social media, consuming every kind of information adds value to our lives or not? Does it help us get closer to our goals or not?

We crave novelty, new stories and new things so we get hooked up to it very easily. Its like delicious fast-food for the brain. It is unhealthy but we don’t want to stop eating it. A split brain, devoid of the force of focus, can rarely accomplish anything.

The term deep work is borrowed from the book of the same name by Cal-Newport, an associate professor of Computer science most the ideas that follow have been heavily influenced by his book and some personal reflections. Deep work is the ability to work without distraction for an extended length of time. Deep work demands a huge degree of isolation from people around you and from social media i.e. distraction.

A quote “let the ripples of the mind calm down, the picture will become clear” often rings bells with me.  We are so much engrossed in the world that we don’t let our minds moments of calmness. We feed it so many ideas that it doesn’t have the energy to come with its own.

Below I discuss strategies for academic productivity. Yet the effectiveness of any strategy depends on the discipline with which we implement it. Taking control of our lives, our thoughts and our goals is what is needed and when we fail to take control some we don’t throw the responsibility on outward factors, (of course this is not relevant to some unavoidable external circumstances like health etc.)

  - A life lived with focus has a sense of direction and meaning. You chose what to focus on. It makes you feel in control about your life. A deeply focused life is well accomplished and happy life.
  - Time is the only finite resource. Focus on the things which are most important to you.
  - Use 20/80 law. 80 percent of the output comes from the 20 percent of the input. Your success majorly comes from 20% of the things you do. So, you should focus more on that 20% of the things. You can use this rule recursively to identify the most important contributors to your success and divide your time accordingly.
  - The mind is a muscle. Mozart was a prodigy but Beethoven refined himself with labour, practice and discipline. The mind is a muscle we can stretch and make it stronger.
  - Do not google every answer, do not look at solved solutions unless you have spent a reasonable amount of time solving it and getting frustrated. Try to give yourself a day of thought for a complicated problem before looking it up online. When you are unable to solve a problem it isn’t that you are wasting time, you are figuring out every wrong way to solve the problem. In solving other problems, it helps a lot.
  - Embrace boredom and frustration as they are part of growth and learning process. We become bored when the brain doesn’t want to do the energetically intensive process of learning and reasoning.
  - When a solution is wrong, make sure you understand why that particular solution was wrong. What assumptions which you took were wrong. Analyzing wrong answers can often lead to greater insights and helps you avoid pitfalls.
  - Block out social media when you work. Ask your family and friends to respect it. Turn off your phone or keep it somewhere away. There are also apps which can block access to apps for some time you set.
  - Whenever you have to visualize some mathematical graphs, some data or anything else, try to visualize it with your mind before plotting it on a calculator. This exercise helps you focus and makes it quicker to come with insights about that particular problem.
  - Ask yourself a question, better put it on your desk, “**What is the best use of time at this moment”?**
  - Make a daily list of ToDo’s Write in on a small notebook you can carry around in your pocket or perhaps wallet. Squeeze menial things like ATM, paying bills groceries, hanging out with friends, commuting together. Plan your day when you will probably do it.
  - Plan ahead but also be flexible about your schedule. Unseen things often pop up as well. Keep room for unexpected things in your schedule.
  - If you are learning a skill, practice it daily for at least 40 minutes. Make it easy to practice e.g. if you are learning to programme, an app that can teach you programming on your phone is a great way to use time.
  - Sleep properly, take one nap during the day at least.
  - During a lecture when you don’t understand anything, ask questions. Clear the gaps as soon as possible. Make that particular concept clear in your head and notes before it wanes off from the mind in the smudged state.
  - Meditate on your particular problem seriously it helps.
  - When you are working on some idea, always identify the assumptions and question them. What if that particular property or that particular lemma doesn’t hold. It’s the beginning of creativity.
  - If you are stuck on a problem, do brainstorming. List all the options, the information you need to proceed to the next step no matter how weird it is. Once a list of possible solutions is in front of us we can easily narrow down the right solution.
  - Set artificial deadlines and complete your job within those deadlines. Use the rest of the time to review and revise. It is much more efficient and easy to start crudely and revise later than to produce a gem-like piece of work first time
  - Exercise properly, make your diet well balanced. Don’t ignore the role of minerals in your diet. They perform crucial functions in the body. Lack of certain minerals can lead to mental exhaustion.
  - Use coffee but do not depend too much on it. There are ways you can not get addicted to coffee.
  - Learn some artistic skill to give your brain some rest. Learn to sculpt, play music, draw, dance, sing whatever your inner being desires. Art gives us pleasure.
  - Rest and reflect about big questions of life. Thinking about big questions helps create a narrative of life. It helps us to realize our role in this world and cosmos.

These are some reflections, which I try to implement in my own life. Sometimes I fail, procrastinate but then again, we are all humans. Use whatever suits you. A productive, well balanced and happy life is what we all need.
