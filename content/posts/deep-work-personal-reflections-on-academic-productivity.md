In a world full of distractions, our mind and attention are split between so many ideas that we are rarely able to garner a force of thought to pierce a specific concept to depth, to reveal its nitty-gritty details. With the advent of social media one click away and cheap entertainment, we cannot stop ourselves from getting hooked to our electronic screens. It doesn’t mean that these things are inherently bad. They have also brought tremendous positive value to our lives. But have we ever taken a hiatus and reflected on whether spending hours on social media, consuming every kind of information, adds value to our lives or not? Does it help us get closer to our goals or not?

We crave novelty, new stories, and new things, so we get hooked on them very easily. It's like delicious fast food for the brain. It is unhealthy, but we don’t want to stop eating it. A split brain, devoid of the force of focus, can rarely accomplish anything.

---

## What is Deep Work?

The term **"deep work"** is borrowed from the book of the same name by Cal Newport, an associate professor of Computer Science. Most of the ideas that follow have been heavily influenced by his book and some personal reflections.

> **Deep work** is the ability to work without distraction for an extended length of time. Deep work demands a high degree of isolation from the people around you and from social media—i.e., distraction.

A quote, *“Let the ripples of the mind calm down, the picture will become clear,”* often rings a bell with me. We are so engrossed in the world that we don’t give our minds moments of calmness. We feed it so many ideas that it doesn’t have the energy to come up with its own.

## Core Philosophy: Taking Control

Below, I discuss strategies for academic productivity. Yet the effectiveness of any strategy depends on the discipline with which we implement it. Taking control of our lives, our thoughts, and our goals is what is needed. When we fail to take control, we shouldn't throw the responsibility onto outward factors (of course, this is not relevant to unavoidable external circumstances like health, etc.).

A life lived with focus has a sense of direction and meaning. You choose what to focus on. It makes you feel in control of your life. A deeply focused life is a well-accomplished and happy life.

> **Key Takeaway:** Time is our only finite resource. Focus on the things that are most important to you.

## Strategies & Principles for Productivity

### 1. Leverage the 80/20 Rule
Use the **80/20 law** (Pareto Principle): **80 percent** of the output comes from **20 percent** of the input. Your success majorly comes from 20% of the things you do. So, you should focus more on that crucial 20%. You can use this rule recursively to identify the most important contributors to your success and divide your time accordingly.

### 2. Treat the Mind as a Muscle
The mind is a muscle. Mozart was a prodigy, but Beethoven refined himself with labor, practice, and discipline. The mind is a muscle we can stretch and make stronger.

### 3. Sit with Frustration & Avoid Easy Outlets
* **Don't Google every answer immediately:** Do not look at solved solutions unless you have spent a reasonable amount of time trying to solve the problem and getting frustrated.
* **Give it time:** Try to give yourself a day of thought for a complicated problem before looking it up online.
* **Reframe failure:** When you are unable to solve a problem, it isn’t that you are wasting time—you are figuring out every wrong way to solve it, which helps immensely when solving other problems.

### 4. Embrace Boredom
Embrace boredom and frustration, as they are essential parts of the growth and learning process. We become bored when the brain doesn’t want to do the energetically intensive process of learning and reasoning.

### 5. Analyze Your Mistakes
When a solution is wrong, make sure you understand *why* that particular solution was wrong. What assumptions did you make that were incorrect? Analyzing wrong answers can often lead to greater insights and help you avoid future pitfalls.

### 6. Protect Your Focus Environment
* **Block out social media** when you work.
* **Set boundaries:** Ask your family and friends to respect your focus time.
* **Remove physical distractions:** Turn off your phone or keep it in another room.
* **Use technology thoughtfully:** Use app blockers to limit access for set periods.

### 7. Exercise Your Mental Visualization
Whenever you have to visualize mathematical graphs, data, or anything else, try to visualize it in your mind before plotting it on a calculator or software. This exercise strengthens focus and helps you derive quicker insights.

### 8. The Desk Question
Ask yourself this question—or better yet, post it on your desk:

> *"What is the best use of my time at this moment?"*

### 9. Organize and Plan Dynamically
* **Daily To-Do Lists:** Keep a small notebook in your pocket or wallet. Squeeze in menial tasks like visiting the ATM, paying bills, buying groceries, or commuting. Plan when you will execute them.
* **Plan Ahead with Flexibility:** Keep room for the unexpected in your schedule, as unforeseen events always pop up.

### 10. Deliberate Practice & Learning Skills
If you are learning a skill, practice it daily for at least **40 minutes**. Make it easy to practice—for example, if you are learning programming, an interactive mobile app is a great way to utilize spare moments.

### 11. Clarify Concepts Immediately
During a lecture or meeting when you don’t understand something, **ask questions**. Clear gaps as soon as possible. Make that concept clear in your head and notes before it wanes into a smudged state.

### 12. Deep Problem Solving & Mental Habits
* **Meditate on your problems:** Sit seriously with a complex issue.
* **Question assumptions:** When working on an idea, always identify and question your underlying assumptions. What if a particular property or lemma doesn’t hold? That is the beginning of creativity.
* **Brainstorm options:** If you are stuck, list all options and information needed for the next step, no matter how weird. Having a full list makes it easier to narrow down the right solution.
* **Iterate with Artificial Deadlines:** Set artificial deadlines and complete your job within them. Use remaining time to review and revise. It is far more efficient to start crudely and refine later than to try producing a masterpiece on the first pass.

## Rest, Health, and Philosophy

* **Prioritize Sleep & Rest:** Sleep properly, and take at least one short nap during the day.
* **Nourish the Body:** Exercise properly and eat a well-balanced diet. Don’t ignore minerals, as deficiencies can lead to mental exhaustion.
* **Mindful Caffeine Use:** Enjoy coffee, but do not rely heavily on it so as to avoid addiction.
* **Engage in Art:** Learn an artistic skill to give your analytical brain a break—sculpt, play music, draw, dance, or sing. Art brings genuine pleasure and mental recovery.
* **Reflect on the Big Questions:** Rest and reflect on life's bigger questions. Thinking about them creates a coherent narrative for your life and helps reveal your role in this world and the cosmos.

## Conclusion

These are some reflections that I try to implement in my own life. Sometimes I fail or procrastinate, but then again, we are all human. Use whatever strategies suit you best. A productive, well-balanced, and happy life is what we all strive for.
